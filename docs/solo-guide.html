1 · How to use this guide

Crisis Mode is the rulebook's solo mode: it replaces the Game Master with dice oracles and a set of timers. This guide walks the whole procedure in the order you actually play it, and shows the app's controls at each point. Every worked example follows one hero through one complete crisis, from the first alert to going home.

The dice in this guide are real. Each roll was made by the app's own solo engines while this document was generated, using the same code the app runs during play. Nothing was chosen to look tidy — where a roll went badly, the example carries on with the bad result.

Before you start
  • Turn on Settings → Solo play (Crisis Mode). A Solo tab appears in the bottom bar.
  • Build a hero on the Home tab if you have not already. Tick the solo box on the wizard's rank step so the allowance below is applied.
  • Keep the Action tab handy — that is where fights are run when one starts.

The solo build allowance

A solo hero carries the whole story, so the chapter grants a little more:

  • Create your hero normally, then add 2 extra attribute points.
  • Start with one additional free talent. Durable, Resilience and Second Wind suit solo play.
  • Favour powers that support utility and survivability (DUPLICATION, HEALING, QUICKNESS) over powers that mainly aid allies or need GM interpretation (ACTION PLAN, PRECOGNITION).
  • Any rank works as long as you generate matching crisis alerts. Global Guardian is recommended.
  • Instead of end-of-session karma, solo heroes earn karma by completing objectives on the objective timer.
The app applies the extra points and the extra free talent automatically once the solo box is ticked on the rank step.

2 · The loop

Solo play is a six-step loop. The Solo tab prints it as a numbered strip and highlights the step you are on; the card at the top of the tab always names the single next action.

StepWhat it isThe app control
1Generate a crisis alert from the random threat tables or the Complex Response Engine.Generate crisis alert
2Start the crisis level at 0 and begin making event checks.Event check
3Choose a crisis and start a crisis timer, plus any ally, objective or encounter timers.Engage, on the Crises card
4Make checks, track timers and the crisis level, and engage threats or opportunities.Say what your hero just did

The one rule that keeps the loop moving

Always have at least one timer running. A timer is what makes the situation change while you are busy — it is the pressure a GM would otherwise apply. If every timer is stopped, nothing is pushing back and play stalls.

Crisis level

A single 0–10 counter for how bad things have got. It rises when event checks fire and when a crisis timer runs out. It feeds back into the dice: a higher level makes crisis events harsher and objectives harder.

0–3Low dangerNo penalty
4–7Medium danger−1 progress die
8–10High danger−2 progress dice

3 · Steps 1 and 2 — the alert, then the heartbeat

Step 1 · Generate a crisis alert

A crisis alert works like a briefing: a priority message from an agency, a breaking news story, or something your hero witnesses on patrol.

Press Generate crisis alert. The app rolls the threat tables that suit your rank and lays the result out as four separate facts — kind, what is happening, where, and a complication. It also seeds the first entry on the Crises card and resets the crisis level to 0.

If you want the place described

The alert dialog offers the five location engines — City, Region, Space, Atmosphere, Facility. Roll one when you need to know what somewhere is actually like. The Atmosphere engine is blended into the others automatically so a place gets a mood as well as a shape.

Step 2 · Event checks

An event check is the heartbeat of the crisis: 2D6, rolled whenever time passes or you want to know whether the situation turns. It is the main way new trouble arrives.

2D6Result
2Roll for two crisis events; crisis level +2.
3–4Roll for one crisis event; crisis level +1.
5–10No event.
11–12Opportunity event.

A crisis event is rolled on the Crisis Event Engine: D66 picks one of 36 focuses, then 2D6 plus the current crisis level picks the severity band (2-10 / 11-15 / 16+). Because the level is added, an escalating crisis produces harsher events by itself.

4 · Step 3 — choosing a crisis and starting timers

The Crises card lists everything you could engage: the alert seeded one, and every event check that fires adds more. Engage turns a crisis into a running crisis timer named after it. Ignore leaves it pending — ignoring a danger is a legitimate choice, and it will still be there later.

Then add whichever other timers the scene needs. There are four types and they are not interchangeable — each answers a different question, and each is checked at a different moment.

TimerQuestion it answersCheck it when
CrisisHow long until this goes wrong?Time passes — between scenes, on a delay, changing location, lingering. +1 die for anything lengthy, −1 for anything fast.
ObjectiveAm I getting what I came for?A milestone happens — something meaningful for or against the goal. Never on a clock.
AllyHow is the group holding up?They face a threat or try something dangerous. Off-screen, at least once every few hours of game time.
EncounterHave they found me yet?Only while exploring or evading somewhere a fight could break out — once per zone you move through or linger in.
You never have to pick a timer yourself

Step 4's control is Say what your hero just did. Choose from six plain descriptions of what happened — moved somewhere, searched, hit a milestone, allies in danger, a fight ended, time jumped — and the app fires the right checks in the right order with the right modifiers. The table above is the rule it is applying, printed so you can follow along.

The session so far

Backdraft engages the reactor crisis. It becomes a crisis timer: The reactor floods the lower decks, starting at distant because the crisis level is still low.

She also sets an objective — Get the maintenance crew out — at Far off: 2 progress dice, paying 4 karma when reached.

Nothing rolls yet. Setting an objective is bookkeeping; it only advances on a milestone.

6 · The objective timer

Your goal, and in solo play your only source of karma — the ten end-of-session questions do not apply. Name the objective, give it a starting distance, and it pays out when reached.

StatusProgress diceKarma if reached
Out of reach16
Far off24
Manageable32
Near41
Within reach5—
Achievable6—
Objective reached——

Something further away is slower but pays more. Roll its dice only on a milestone — a development that genuinely moved the goal, for or against. Never on a timer.

The arithmetic
  • Each 6 advances one step; each 1 cancels a 6.
  • A net negative result pushes the objective one step back, and overcoming that setback becomes your next milestone.
  • A medium crisis costs −1 die, a high crisis −2.

7 · The encounter timer

Tracks how close the opposition is getting. Only start one when it applies: your hero is moving through or searching somewhere they could be found, and it is uncertain whether they have been. A fixed scene you already understand — holding up a collapsing floor, talking someone down, fighting what is already in front of you — needs no encounter timer at all.

The Encounter panel has a Do I need one right now? check that asks exactly that question.

Avoiding a fight

  • Hide: decide whether the enemy actively searches (ask the Binary Engine). If they search, roll INTUITION for them; if they don't search or fail, they move out of the zone within minutes.
  • Back out: you can retreat from the zone without risk, but consider what that costs you in the location.
  • Sneak past: roll INTUITION for the enemy; on a failure you slip through to the next zone.

Escaping one

Escape is an AGILITY roll. The modifiers:

SituationDice
You are using a movement power well suited to the environment+1
You are outnumbered-1

You are escaping a speedy enemy such as a Viltrumite-2
Hostile environment such as fires or extreme cold-2

The twelve-step encounter sequence

The app walks this for you — the Encounter panel shows which step you are on and offers only that step's controls. It is printed here so you can see where you are in the procedure.

  1. Choose your movement mode: alert, cautious or rushed.
  2. Make an encounter check with enemy dice for the current presence, modified by movement mode.
  3. Each 6 shifts the enemy presence one step closer.
  4. If the presence isn't 'encountered' yet, continue moving.
  5. On 'encountered', check the enemy behaviour table for who spots whom.
  6. Check the enemy threat table (or established facts) for what you're facing.
  7. Make spotting rolls as needed. An unspotted side may reveal itself, ambush, back out or hide.
  8. If spotted but not surprised, you may attempt to escape with an AGILITY roll.
  9. If the encounter isn't avoided or escaped, draw initiative.
  10. Once resolved, reset the encounter timer.
  11. Check any active crisis timers, modified by movement mode and prolonged actions.
  12. Repeat as you move on or linger.

9 · The oracles

Everything that answers a question a GM would otherwise answer. They live on the Ask the oracles card.

Yes / no — the Binary Response Engine

D6Answer
1Strong no
2–3No
4–5Yes
6Strong yes

If a yes is likely, roll 2D6 and keep the highest; if a no is likely, keep the lowest. A strong result is unequivocal and hands you something extra besides.

Complex answers

Two D66 rolls give a directive and a subject — for example Abandon / Authority, which might mean the officer in charge walks off the job. Take it literally, interpret it, or roll again.

Describe a place

Five location engines, rolled when you arrive somewhere and need to know what it is like:

  • City Engine — Where in the city you are, or need to travel to.
  • Region Engine — Outdoor locations and larger regions.
  • Space Engine — Outer space and interstellar travel.
  • Facility Engine — Interiors of buildings, complexes, bases, lairs and factories.
  • Atmosphere Engine — A descriptive prompt for an area — mundane detail, mystery or obstacle.

Crisis event — the jolt

If you are ever unsure what happens next: raise the crisis level by 1 and roll a crisis event. That is the book's own advice, and the app has a button for it.

10 · Combat

A fight starts in exactly three ways
  1. An encounter timer reaches Encountered and you neither avoid nor escape it. The Encounter panel walks you to Draw initiative.
  2. You choose to attack something the fiction has already put in front of you — start an action scene on the Action tab.
  3. A crisis timer fires into a fight, because that is what you named it as triggering.

Combat itself is the ordinary combat system — the Action tab runs it. Initiative is a card 1–10 per combatant per round, lowest first. On your turn you have one full action plus one quick action, or two quick actions.

The app deals the cards, marks whose turn it is, and gates attacking on turn order. Attacking spends your full action and ends your turn, and the marker moves to the next card automatically.

Running the enemy without a GM

The encounter roll already told you two things: behaviour (from the highest die) and threat size (from the number of 6s). Use behaviour to decide what they do on their turn, and ask the Binary Engine when it is genuinely unclear. Pull the actual stat block from the NPCs tab.

Powers without a GM

  • With a relevant power such as DETECTION, ENHANCED SENSES or TELEPATHY, an encounter check that rolls at least one 6 and no more than one 1 without triggering an encounter lets you choose one option instead of rolling the next check.
  • Outmaneuver: if the presence is closing or worse, shift it one rank further away as you move to an adjacent zone.
  • Prepare: if the presence is closing or near, shift straight to encountered and ignore the behaviour table — you automatically spot them and they must roll INTUITION to spot you.

Solo combat and recovery

  • Draw initiative as normal for your hero and any NPCs; a single card covers an NPC group.
  • Roll for NPC actions as a GM would when they act against or aid your hero. Some push rolls and take stress for exploits; many won't.
  • Choose the most sensible NPC action for the circumstances. If unsure, ask the Binary Engine.
  • When an NPC does something that doesn't directly affect your hero, just decide whether it works — unless it's dramatic and dangerous.
  • Most sentient NPCs value self-preservation: when things go badly they defend or flee. Check their willingness with the Binary Engine.
  • When an NPC is broken you need not roll a critical injury — declare them knocked out or killed as fits the moment.
  • If you are broken by damage or stress you can still act, at -1 die.
  • When broken by stress you may make a PRESENCE roll as if aided by an ally — but the aid is an important memory. Decide what comforts or inspires you in that moment.

The app shows the recovery panel exactly when it applies, including a Rally on a memory roll at PRESENCE with a help die.

11 · Step 5 — the social scene

After you resolve an event, a threat or an objective, play a social scene. Mechanically it restores Resolve equal to your PRESENCE rating — and Resolve does not come back from resting, so this is the only way to get it.

  • Condense a social scene into a brief statement rather than roleplaying a conversation with yourself.
  • Alternatively, frame it as a flashback relevant to the current crisis.
  • The social hooks table works as inspiration.

The app's Social scene button applies the recovery, prints that guidance and offers a social-hooks roll for a prompt. It also clears the "social scene due" state, so the loop moves on.

When it is due

The Solo tab tracks this for you: resolving a crisis, completing an objective, or a timer firing all mark a social scene as due, and the next-step card will say so. Playing one through the ordinary session lifecycle counts too.

12 · Step 6 — ending the crisis

Resolve crisis

When the danger is dealt with, press Resolve crisis. It shows exactly what it will do first — clear the alert, drop pending crises, stop the running timers, reset the crisis level, queue a social scene — and it is undoable in one step.

Head home

With nothing left running and nothing waiting, Head home closes the session:

  • A few hours' rest: all Health and all Resolve return.
  • Every reached objective pays its karma. This is where solo karma is actually banked.
  • Karma spending unlocks — it is spent between sessions, in a safe location, and home counts.
  • The board clears, ready for the next alert.
Karma in solo play

Solo heroes do not answer the ten earn / six bad-karma session questions — objectives replace them. With Crisis Mode on, the app's End session dialog suppresses those questions and points you back here, so the two paths can never double-count.

13 · Transitions at a glance

The single hardest part of solo play is knowing when to move from one thing to the next. This is the whole answer on one page.

Deciding whether you need an encounter timer

Ask one question: is my hero moving through or searching somewhere the opposition could find them? Patrol, sweep of an unknown building, sneaking past a cordon, an escape — yes. A fixed, known scene — no, and the crisis timers and objective carry on regardless.

If you are stuck

  1. Check the next-step card at the top of the Solo tab. It always names one action.
  2. If the fiction has stalled rather than the procedure, take the jolt: crisis level +1 and roll a crisis event.
  3. If a specific question is blocking you, ask the Binary Engine and accept the answer.

Appendix C · One-page play aid

The loop

  1. Generate a crisis alert from the random threat tables or the Complex Response Engine.
  2. Start the crisis level at 0 and begin making event checks.
  3. Choose a crisis and start a crisis timer, plus any ally, objective or encounter timers.
  4. Make checks, track timers and the crisis level, and engage threats or opportunities.
  5. After resolving an event, threat or objective, play a social scene — especially to recover Resolve.
  6. Return to any remaining crisis dangers, or head home to rest, recover and earn karma.
